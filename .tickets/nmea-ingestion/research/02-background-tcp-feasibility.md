# 02 — Background TCP feasibility: research findings

Ticket: [`issues/02-background-tcp-feasibility.md`](../issues/02-background-tcp-feasibility.md)
Researched: 2026-08-08 · Target: Expo SDK 55.0.4 / RN 0.83.2 / Android only

---

## VERDICT: **PASSED WITH CAVEATS**

A 3-hour, screen-off, backgrounded TCP capture is achievable on this app's exact
stack. The mechanism is an **Android foreground service of type
`connectedDevice`**, which is what keeps the app process out of the cached-apps
freezer — and the freezer is the thing that would otherwise kill the socket:

> "If all processes for a particular app are frozen, the system terminates any
> active TCP sockets maintained by the app."
> — [AOSP, Cached apps freezer](https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/cached-apps-freezer)

Three caveats, in order of how much they should change the design:

1. **JS timers do not survive backgrounding on RN Android.** This is a
   repo-verifiable fact, not folklore:
   `JavaTimerManager.onHostPause()` sets `isPaused = true` and calls
   `clearFrameCallback()`, removing the Choreographer callback that drives
   `setTimeout`/`setInterval`
   (`node_modules/react-native/ReactAndroid/src/main/java/com/facebook/react/modules/core/JavaTimerManager.kt:72-77`).
   Whether Choreographer frames fire at all with the display off is something I
   **could not establish from a primary source** — see
   [Not established](#what-i-could-not-establish). **Therefore: the capture
   pipeline must be event-driven off socket `data` callbacks, and any periodic
   work must be driven by a native tick, not `setInterval`.** This is a design
   constraint on tickets 04/05/09, not just an implementation detail.
2. **Do not use `dataSync`.** Android 15+ caps `dataSync` foreground services at
   **6 hours per 24-hour period**, after which the system calls
   `Service.onTimeout()` and kills the service. A 3-hour race fits, but two
   races in a day may not. `connectedDevice` has no such cap and is the
   semantically correct type anyway.
3. **OEM battery managers are the residual risk.** Samsung/Xiaomi/Huawei
   aggressive power management is the single most common cause of "the
   foreground service died after 40 minutes" reports. The mitigation is a
   one-time user action (exclude the app from battery optimisation), not code.

The gate is passed in the sense that the capture design in the map can proceed
unchanged. It is *not* passed in the sense of "install a library and it works" —
there is no off-the-shelf, maintained, Expo-config-plugin'd foreground-service
package for this stack, and the one obvious candidate is explicitly abandoned by
its author. Budget for owning ~150 lines of Kotlin.

**A device spike is mandatory before ticket 04.** See
[Validation plan](#validation-plan-do-this-before-building-anything).

## 1. The mechanism for staying alive

### 1a. Why a foreground service is genuinely required (the mechanism, not the vibe)

Android 11+ ships a **cached apps freezer**. In Android 14+ it freezes app
processes **10 seconds after entering the cached state**, all threads are
suspended, and — decisively for this ticket — *"If all processes for a
particular app are frozen, the system terminates any active TCP sockets
maintained by the app."*
([AOSP](https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/cached-apps-freezer))

The same page names the escape hatch: *"you can change the process status to
noncached before the process needs to do any work … Examples of active statuses
include a bound foreground service or foreground status."*

Separately, Doze suspends network access, ignores wake locks, and defers jobs
when the device is unplugged, stationary and screen-off
([Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)).
An app "with a process currently in the foreground, either as an activity or
foreground service" is not treated as idle. And the power-management limits
table is explicit: for an app running a foreground service, **network access has
"No restrictions"**
([Power management restrictions](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details)).

So: foreground service ⇒ not cached ⇒ not frozen ⇒ socket survives, and network
is not gated by App Standby. That is the whole mechanism.

### 1b. `expo-task-manager` / `expo-background-task` — **ruled out**

`expo-background-task` uses Android `WorkManager`; the docs state the *"minimum
interval"* is **15 minutes** and describe the unit of work as *"a deferrable
unit of work … performed in the background, outside your app's lifecycle"*
([Expo BackgroundTask docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/background-task/)).
Doze also *"doesn't let `JobScheduler` run (and consequently WorkManager tasks
don't run)"*
([Doze](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)).
Deferrable 15-minute wakeups cannot hold a socket open. `expo-task-manager` is
only the task registry underneath it — useful plumbing, not a keep-alive.

### 1c. `expo-location`'s foreground service — **works in principle, rejected as the primary mechanism**

This was the most interesting option because the dependency is already present.
What I found:

- `expo-location` ships a real Android foreground service, declared in its own
  library manifest as
  `<service android:name=".services.LocationTaskService" android:foregroundServiceType="location" />`
  (`node_modules/expo-location/android/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`).
- It is a plain `Service` that calls `startForeground(...)` with an ongoing
  notification (`LocationTaskService.kt`). It does **not** create a separate JS
  runtime for itself; it exists to hold the app process at foreground
  importance while `LocationTaskConsumer` delivers location callbacks into the
  app's existing JS context.

**So yes — mechanically, its FGS would keep the process non-cached, and an
unrelated TCP socket opened from the app's JS would keep running.** The FGS is a
process-level status; it does not know or care what else the process is doing.

I am recommending against relying on it anyway:

- **It would not fix the JS-timer problem.** A bare `Service` does not re-arm
  the Choreographer callback that RN's timers ride on (see §1e).
- **Lifetime is not ours.** The service lives and dies with the location task.
  Expo has open reports of exactly this service misbehaving over long runs —
  e.g. [expo/expo#47595](https://github.com/expo/expo/issues/47595)
  (background-location FGS freezes/ANRs after an app update, because
  `startForeground` is only called from the async `bindService` callback), and
  [expo/expo#14076](https://github.com/expo/expo/issues/14076) (location stops
  updating after 5–10 minutes on Android with the foreground service).
- **It costs permissions we don't otherwise need.** Turning it on requires
  `isAndroidForegroundServiceEnabled` (and realistically
  `isAndroidBackgroundLocationEnabled` → `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`), which is
  a scary runtime prompt and, per the Expo docs, a Play Store review item.
- **Wrong FGS type.** `location` would be a lie about why the service exists;
  `connectedDevice` is exactly right (§4).

Keep it in the back pocket as a *belt-and-braces* option if the boat's own GPS
turns out to be insufficient and we want device GPS in the track anyway — in
that case running both FGS types is legitimate.

### 1d. `react-native-background-actions` — the fast path, with real caveats

[`react-native-background-actions`](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions),
v**4.1.0** (2026-04-07), 944 stars. Same author as `react-native-tcp-socket`.

What it does, from source
(`android/src/main/java/com/asterinet/react/bgactions/RNBackgroundActionsTask.java`):

- `RNBackgroundActionsTask extends HeadlessJsTaskService`
- `getTaskConfig` → `new HeadlessJsTaskConfig(taskName, args, 0, true)` —
  **timeout 0 (no timeout), `allowedInForeground = true`**
- `onStartCommand` → `ServiceCompat.startForeground(this, id, notification, bgOptions.getForegroundServiceType())`
- implements both `onTimeout(int)` and `onTimeout(int, int)` → `stopSelf()`
  (i.e. it will surrender gracefully to the Android 15 `dataSync` cap rather
  than ANR)

`foregroundServiceType` at runtime **only landed in 4.1.0**
([release notes](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/releases/tag/v4.1.0),
[#265](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/265)).
Before that, targetSdk ≥ 34 apps crashed with
`InvalidForegroundServiceTypeException`
([#264](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/264)).

Why this is attractive: because it is a `HeadlessJsTaskService` with an active
task, `JavaTimerManager.onHeadlessJsTaskStart()` calls `setChoreographerCallback()`
— i.e. it is the one option that *re-arms RN's JS timers while backgrounded*
(`JavaTimerManager.kt:91-95`). And `HeadlessJsTaskService` reuses the app's
existing React instance, so a socket opened in the normal app JS context stays
addressable from the task.

Why I would not make it load-bearing:

- **The maintainer has publicly stepped away.** 2026-04-07, on the New
  Architecture issue: *"I am no longer active in the ReactNative ecosystem, so
  any PR is more than welcome!"*
  ([#249](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/249)).
- **New Architecture support is unverified.**
  [#249](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/249)
  is open since 2024-10 with no maintainer answer;
  [#224](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/224)
  ("Service doesn't work with new architecture" — notification appears, task
  never registers) was closed as a duplicate, not as fixed. Community consensus
  in the thread is that RN ≥ 0.76 fixed the underlying RN bug, but nobody has
  confirmed on 0.8x. React Native Directory reports
  `newArchitecture: false` with the modifier *"New Architecture support
  unknown"* — i.e. undeclared, not known-broken.
- **No Expo config plugin.** The manifest edit is mandatory and this repo runs
  `expo prebuild --clean`. A plugin exists only as an unmerged PR:
  [#268](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/pulls/268)
  (2026-05-10). We'd write our own either way — which is most of the work of
  owning the module outright.
- **Its library manifest declares `<service android:name=".RNBackgroundActionsTask"/>`
  with no `foregroundServiceType`**, so our plugin must merge the attribute onto
  a service we don't own (`tools:node="merge"`), which is exactly the pattern
  that produced the empty-`foregroundServiceType` EAS bug
  ([expo/eas-cli#2556](https://github.com/expo/eas-cli/issues/2556)).
- Long-run field reports are mixed at best:
  [#250](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/250)
  — *"randomly closing when the screen is locked … sometimes works 3-4 hrs;
  sometimes not even 20 min"*, and *"`dataSync` then max 40 minutes"*.
  Note these are pre-4.1.0 reports using the wrong FGS type, on OEM-managed
  devices — but they are the closest thing to evidence about our exact workload.

Verdict on this library: **excellent for the spike, questionable for v1.** Use it
to answer "does a socket survive 3 hours screen-off on Logan's actual phone?" in
an afternoon. If the answer is yes, port the ~150 lines into a local module we
own.

### 1e. Purpose-written local Expo module — **recommended**

The app already prebuilds and ships a custom dev client, so
`npx create-expo-module --local` gives a first-class Kotlin module under
`modules/` with no npm dependency and no third-party manifest to merge against.

What it needs to do, and why each part:

| Responsibility | Why |
| --- | --- |
| `Service` subclass declared with `android:foregroundServiceType="connectedDevice"` | Keeps the process non-cached ⇒ socket not terminated by the freezer |
| Extend `HeadlessJsTaskService` with `HeadlessJsTaskConfig(name, args, 0, true)` | Re-arms RN's JS timers via `JavaTimerManager.onHeadlessJsTaskStart` (`JavaTimerManager.kt:91`); also reuses the app's existing React instance |
| Hold a `PowerManager.PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK` for the duration | CPU stays up with the screen off; Doze ignores wake locks but an FGS process is not idle |
| Emit a **native periodic tick** from a Kotlin `ScheduledExecutorService` | Removes JS `setInterval` from the critical path entirely (§1f). ~10 lines. |
| `onTimeout(startId, fgsType)` → stop cleanly + surface an error to JS | Defensive; `connectedDevice` shouldn't time out, but never ANR |
| Start only from a user tap while the app is foregrounded | Android 12+ blocks background FGS starts (`ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException`); RNBA hits this too and swallows it |

This is a small, well-understood amount of Kotlin, and it puts the one
load-bearing piece of the feature under our control rather than an abandoned
dependency. `efstathiosntonas/expo-foreground-service`
([GitHub](https://github.com/efstathiosntonas/expo-foreground-service), 2 stars,
5 commits, Sep 2025, explicitly *"I am too lazy to create an npm module for
this so just copy paste in your `modules` folder"*) is a usable **reference** for
the shape — not a dependency.

### 1f. The JS-timer trap (read this before designing the capture loop)

From this repo's own `node_modules`
(`react-native/ReactAndroid/src/main/java/com/facebook/react/modules/core/JavaTimerManager.kt`):

```kotlin
override fun onHostPause() {
  isPaused.set(true)
  clearFrameCallback()      // removes the Choreographer TIMERS_EVENTS callback
  maybeIdleCallback()
}
...
override fun onHeadlessJsTaskStart(taskId: Int) {
  if (!isRunningTasks.getAndSet(true)) {
    setChoreographerCallback()   // re-arms it
    ...
  }
}
```

RN's `setTimeout`/`setInterval` are driven by `ReactChoreographer`, which wraps
`android.view.Choreographer` on the **main (UI) thread**
(`ReactChoreographer.kt:10,25,107`). Two consequences:

1. Backgrounding the app (`onHostPause`) stops JS timers unless a Headless JS
   task is active. The RN docs corroborate the general shape: *"React Native will
   go into 'paused' mode … unless there are other tasks running, or there is a
   foreground app"*
   ([Headless JS](https://reactnative.dev/docs/headless-js-android)).
2. Even with the callback re-armed, whether Choreographer delivers frames with
   the **display off** is unproven either way (see
   [Not established](#what-i-could-not-establish)). The whole
   `react-native-background-timer` genre of libraries exists because of this.

**Design implication, and it is a real one:** the NMEA pipeline must be driven by
socket `data` events, which arrive via the native module's reader thread → the
JS thread's message queue, and are *not* vsync-gated. Batching, flushing to
SQLite, staleness detection, and reconnect backoff must all hang off either
(a) sentence arrival counts, or (b) the native tick from §1e. **No `setInterval`
anywhere in the capture path.** Ticket 05 (sample rate) and ticket 11
(connection UX) should be written with this in mind.

Note the maintained-timer options are poor: `ocetnik/react-native-background-timer`
last pushed **2023-10-25** with **129 open issues**; the only New-Arch fork,
`@alimirzayev/react-native-background-timer`, was created 2026-07-13 with 1 star.
Neither is worth a dependency when 10 lines of Kotlin in a module we already own
does the job.

## 2. The TCP client library

**[`react-native-tcp-socket`](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket)
v6.4.2 — recommended, with eyes open.**

**Maintained?** Yes, currently. v6.4.2 shipped **2026-07-26** with three
community fixes, including an Android `NullPointerException` in `write()` from a
TOCTOU race with `destroy()`
([#233/#234](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket/pull/234)).
~66k weekly npm downloads, 392 stars, 27 open issues, not archived. Caveat: same
author as §1d, who has said he's left the RN ecosystem — recent releases are
merged community PRs, which is a fragile but functioning state.

**Does it work on RN 0.83 / New Arch?** Best available evidence says **yes**, and
it is unusually good evidence. PR
[#232](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket/pull/232)
(2026-05-17, closed unmerged) is a detailed report from someone who ran the
library on *"Android RN 0.83 (New Arch / bridgeless / Hermes) … device-proven on
a physical device (Nokia 8.3 5G, arm64-v8a)"*. The library connected and
streamed; the defects that PR fixes only surface under js-libp2p volume — per-chunk
`folly::dynamic` allocation through `RCTDeviceEventEmitter` causing OOM under
*"libp2p volume"* (millions of nodes/second-scale churn). **NMEA 0183 at ~1–10 Hz
of short ASCII lines is four to six orders of magnitude below that.** The
official New-Arch request,
[#187](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket/issues/187), has been
open since 2024-04 with zero maintainer comments; React Native Directory reports
`newArchitecture: false` / *"New Architecture support unknown"*.

The reason it works despite never being ported: it is a classic bridge module
(`TcpSocketModule extends ReactContextBaseJavaModule`, no `codegenConfig` in
`package.json`), and RN still runs those through the **interop layer**. From the
0.82 release notes: *"We will keep the interop layers in the codebase for the
foreseeable future. All the classes and functions that are required by the
interop layers won't be removed anytime soon … We've also verified that the 3P
libraries that offer backward compatibility with both architectures will keep on
working with 0.82 where New Architecture is the only architecture."*
([RN 0.82](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/10/08/react-native-0.82)).
RN 0.83 is explicitly a no-breaking-changes release
([RN 0.83](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/12/10/react-native-0.83)).

**Does it keep the socket open inside a foreground service?** The library has no
opinion about services — it runs socket I/O on its own
`Executors.newFixedThreadPool(2)` inside the app process
(`TcpSocketModule.java:52`). Socket survival is therefore purely a question of
whether the *process* survives, which is §1's job. Note there is no
per-connection thread: **N_THREADS = 2** for all sockets, which is fine for one
NMEA stream but worth remembering if the simulator (ticket 12) ever opens many.

**Two features that matter for this specific use case:**

- `interface: 'wifi' | 'ethernet' | 'cellular'` on `connect()` — **Android only**
  ([README](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket#createconnection)).
  This is important. The Zeus 3's WiFi network has no internet; Android will
  keep the default route on cellular and may prompt "this network has no
  internet access". Pinning the socket to the WiFi network removes an entire
  class of "works at the marina, fails at sea" bug. **Use it.**
- `localAddress` — the README says it is *"highly recommended to specify a
  `localAddress` to prevent overload errors and improve performance"*.

**No Expo config plugin, and none needed** — it's autolinked and needs only
`INTERNET`, which `app.config.js` already produces. Its README asks for
`minSdkVersion = 21`; we're on 24.

**Alternatives considered:** nothing credible. WebSocket won't speak raw NMEA
TCP. Writing the socket natively inside the foreground service module is the
*hardening* option if `react-native-tcp-socket` disappoints — see
[Risks](#risks).

## 3. New Architecture status

**The app runs the New Architecture.** `android/gradle.properties:38` →
`newArchEnabled=true`, Hermes on, RN 0.83.2. Since RN 0.82 the New Architecture
is the *only* architecture — you cannot opt out
([RN 0.82](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/10/08/react-native-0.82)).

This does **not** eliminate either candidate, because both are legacy bridge
modules running through the interop layer, which RN has committed to keeping
"for the foreseeable future" (quoted in §2). Concretely:

| Library | Module style | Codegen | RN Directory | Real-world evidence on 0.8x New Arch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `react-native-tcp-socket` 6.4.2 | `ReactContextBaseJavaModule` | none | `newArchitecture: false` ("unknown") | **Strong** — [#232](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket/pull/232) device-proven on RN 0.83 New Arch / bridgeless / Hermes |
| `react-native-background-actions` 4.1.0 | `ReactContextBaseJavaModule` + `HeadlessJsTaskService` | none | `newArchitecture: false` ("unknown") | **Weak** — [#224](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/224) reported broken on 0.75 bridgeless; community says RN 0.76.1 fixed it; nobody has confirmed on 0.8x |
| Local Expo module | Expo Modules API | n/a | n/a | New-Arch native by construction |

The RN Directory `newArchitecture: false` on both is a *declaration* flag, not a
test result — the accompanying modifier is literally "New Architecture support
unknown". Do not read it as "broken".

The interop layer is the thing to watch on future RN upgrades: when RN
eventually removes it, both bridge modules break at once. That's an argument for
the local module (§1e) and, if `react-native-tcp-socket` ever stalls, for moving
the socket native too.

## 4. Android 14+ mandatory `foregroundServiceType`

**Use `connectedDevice`. Not `dataSync`.** This is not a close call.

The official type table describes `connectedDevice` as:

> "Interactions with external devices that require a Bluetooth, NFC, IR, USB, or
> **network connection**."
> — [Foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)

"Reading data from a boat instrument over WiFi" is precisely that. `dataSync` is
described as *"data upload or download, backup-and-restore … transfer data
between a device and the cloud over a network"* — a sync, which this is not.

**Runtime prerequisite for `connectedDevice`** (at least one must hold): declare
one of `CHANGE_NETWORK_STATE`, `CHANGE_WIFI_STATE`, `CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE`,
`NFC`, `TRANSMIT_IR`; **or** be granted one of the Bluetooth/UWB permissions;
**or** call `UsbManager.requestPermission()`. `CHANGE_WIFI_STATE` is a normal
(install-time) permission — no runtime prompt — and is trivially justifiable.
Declare it.

**The decisive practical difference — Android 15's `dataSync` cap:**

> "**6 hours** total per 24-hour period for all `dataSync` foreground services
> combined. After 6 hours the system calls `Service.onTimeout(int, int)` … If
> the service doesn't stop: `RemoteServiceException: A foreground service of
> type dataSync did not stop within its timeout`. … Timer resets when the user
> brings the app to the foreground."
> — [Android 15 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)

targetSdk is 36, so this applies. One 3-hour race fits; a two-race day plus a
delivery sail plausibly does not, and the failure mode is the recording dying
mid-race. `connectedDevice` carries **no documented timeout**. This is very
likely the real explanation behind the field reports in
[RNBA #250](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/250)
("`dataSync` then max 40 minutes" is more likely OEM killing, but the 6h cap is a
guaranteed ceiling regardless).

Also required, per the same docs:

- `<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE" />`
- `<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CONNECTED_DEVICE" />`
- `android:foregroundServiceType="connectedDevice"` on the `<service>` element
  (the manifest attribute and the `startForeground` type argument must match)

**Play Store:** apps targeting Android 14+ must declare FGS types in the Play
Console (*Policy > App content*). This app is `distribution: internal` across all
three EAS profiles (`eas.json`) and is not on the Play Store, so the policy
review is not currently a gate. `connectedDevice` is also the type that would
survive review if that ever changes — another reason not to pick `dataSync` for
convenience.

**Not `shortService`** (hard ~3-minute cap) and **not `specialUse`** (needs a
Play Console justification).

## 5. Doze, battery optimisation, and WifiLock over 3 hours

### Doze — largely a non-issue behind an FGS

Doze engages when the device is *"unplugged, stationary, screen off, unused for a
long period"* and exits when *"the user wakes the device by moving it"*
([Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)).
Two independent reasons it shouldn't bite:

1. A phone on a racing yacht is not stationary. (Don't rely on this alone — a
   phone wedged in a bunk on a steady offshore leg might qualify.)
2. Doze's idle determination explicitly excludes an app that *"has a process
   currently in the foreground, either as an activity or foreground service"*.
   The power-management table is unambiguous: with a foreground service,
   **Network = "No restrictions"**
   ([Power details](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details)).

Compare the Restricted App Standby bucket, where **Network = Disabled** — that's
what we're avoiding by holding an FGS.

Do still hold a `PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK` inside the service (`WAKE_LOCK` permission).
Doze "ignores wake locks", but an FGS process isn't in the Doze-idle set, and the
wake lock is what stops the CPU suspending between packets.

### WifiLock — **do not bother. It is dead API.**

I checked the current AOSP source rather than trusting blog posts
([`WifiManager.java`, AOSP `main`](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Wifi/+/refs/heads/main/framework/java/android/net/wifi/WifiManager.java)):

| Constant | Status | Javadoc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `WIFI_MODE_FULL` | `@Deprecated` | *"This API is non-functional and will have no impact."* |
| `WIFI_MODE_SCAN_ONLY` | `@Deprecated` | *"This API is non-functional and will have no impact."* |
| `WIFI_MODE_FULL_HIGH_PERF` | `@Deprecated` | *"automatically replaced with `WIFI_MODE_FULL_LOW_LATENCY` with all the restrictions documented on that lock"* |
| `WIFI_MODE_FULL_LOW_LATENCY` | current | *"The lock is only active when the screen is on. The lock is only active when the acquiring app is running in the foreground."* |

Read that last row again: **the only functioning WifiLock mode is by definition
inactive when the screen is off.** There is no WifiLock that does what the ticket
asks. Drop it from the design.

The good news is you don't need one. Modern Android keeps WiFi associated with
the screen off (the old "Keep Wi-Fi on during sleep" setting is long gone), and
the FGS keeps the app out of the network-restricted buckets. What you *will* get
is WiFi power-save between beacons — added latency of tens to low hundreds of
milliseconds and slightly bursty delivery. **Irrelevant for a 1–10 Hz NMEA
stream, but relevant to how you timestamp samples: stamp at parse time and treat
inter-sentence gaps as jitter, don't assume even spacing.** Worth a line in
ticket 05.

### The actual battery risk: OEM power management

This, not AOSP Doze, is what kills long foreground services in the wild. The
RNBA issue tracker is a museum of it:
[#176](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/176)
— maintainer: *"your OS' battery management might be closing the background
tasks … you might have to set up an exception for unrestricted battery usage"*;
[#125](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/125)
— works over USB, dies 5 minutes after screen-off on battery, fixed only by
disabling the OEM power saver;
[#250](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/250)
— *"sometimes it works 3-4 hrs; sometimes not even 20 min"*.

Mitigations, all cheap:

1. **One-time onboarding step**: send the user to
   `Settings.ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` (or the app's battery
   settings) before their first recording, and check
   `PowerManager.isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()` at capture start; refuse or
   loudly warn if false. Per
   [Power details](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details),
   manual unrestriction *"supersedes app standby bucket limits"*.
2. Tell the user to plug in. A 3-hour socket + SQLite writes + screen off is
   modest, but a phone on a boat should be on a power bank anyway.
3. Make the recording **crash-tolerant**: append to SQLite continuously (which
   the map already implies), and on app launch detect an un-ended session and
   offer to resume/close it. If the OS kills us at hour 2, we lose hour 3, not
   hours 1–2. This is a design requirement, not a nicety — see
   [Risks](#risks).

Thermal/battery measurement over 3 hours is already listed as "Not yet
specified" in [`map.md`](../map.md); the spike below is the natural place to get
a first number.

## 6. The honest fallback

Ranked, if the spike fails or proves unreliable:

1. **Screen-on with `expo-keep-awake` + dimmed brightness.** Already available
   (`expo-keep-awake@55.0.4` is in `node_modules`), zero new native code, and it
   sidesteps *both* the freezer and the JS-timer problem in one move — with the
   activity resumed, `onHostResume` keeps timers armed and the process is
   foreground by definition. Logan has said this isn't viable, and he's right
   that it's poor UX. But note it degrades gracefully: an "always-on capture
   screen" showing live sentence counts is a legitimate, even desirable, UI for
   a recording session, and it's the fallback that costs nothing to keep as an
   option. Recommended as a **user-selectable "high reliability mode"** even if
   the FGS works.
2. **FGS + accept lossy recovery.** Keep the foreground service, but design for
   it dying: persist continuously, auto-restart capture when the app is next
   foregrounded, and stitch sessions. Turns a hard failure into a data gap. This
   should be built *regardless* — it's cheap insurance.
3. **Move the socket and the parser fully native.** A Kotlin reader thread
   inside the service writing straight to SQLite, with JS only for control and
   display. Removes the JS runtime, the interop layer, RN timers and
   `react-native-tcp-socket` from the reliability path in one go. Significantly
   more work (NMEA parsing in Kotlin, a second SQLite writer to coordinate with
   Drizzle) and it fights the app's whole architecture — but it is the answer
   if the JS path proves flaky.
4. **A separate always-on recorder.** A cheap Android device or a Raspberry Pi
   left aboard, exporting a log the app imports. Kills the on-the-water UX
   (tickets 09/11 largely evaporate) and adds hardware. Last resort.

**Not recommended:** `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`-based tricks (Android 15 now requires
a *visible* overlay window to start an FGS from the background —
[Android 15 changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)),
or `AlarmManager` polling (Doze defers alarms, and you can't hold a socket
between them).

### `app.config.js` changes

Two changes. Nothing else in the file needs to move.

**1. Add a local config plugin to `plugins`:**

```js
plugins: [
  // ...existing entries unchanged...
  './plugins/withNmeaForegroundService',
],
```

**2. Create `plugins/withNmeaForegroundService.js`:**

```js
const { withAndroidManifest, AndroidConfig } = require('expo/config-plugins');

const PERMISSIONS = [
  'android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE',
  'android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CONNECTED_DEVICE',
  'android.permission.WAKE_LOCK',
  // runtime prerequisite that legitimises the connectedDevice FGS type
  'android.permission.CHANGE_WIFI_STATE',
  'android.permission.ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE',
  // so the ongoing notification is visible in the drawer on Android 13+
  'android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS',
];

// Fully-qualified name of the Service in the local Expo module.
const SERVICE = 'com.loganmartlew.sailplan.nmea.NmeaCaptureService';

module.exports = function withNmeaForegroundService(config) {
  config = AndroidConfig.Permissions.withPermissions(config, PERMISSIONS);

  return withAndroidManifest(config, (cfg) => {
    const app = AndroidConfig.Manifest.getMainApplicationOrThrow(cfg.modResults);
    app.service = app.service ?? [];
    const existing = app.service.find((s) => s.$['android:name'] === SERVICE);
    const attrs = {
      'android:name': SERVICE,
      'android:exported': 'false',
      'android:stopWithTask': 'false',
      'android:foregroundServiceType': 'connectedDevice',
    };
    if (existing) Object.assign(existing.$, attrs);
    else app.service.push({ $: attrs });
    return cfg;
  });
};
```

If you spike with `react-native-background-actions` first, point `SERVICE` at
`com.asterinet.react.bgactions.RNBackgroundActionsTask` and add
`'tools:node': 'merge'` to `attrs` (the library declares that service in its own
manifest without a type). Verify the merged
`android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml` after `expo prebuild` — the
`foregroundServiceType` attribute has been observed getting blanked in EAS builds
([eas-cli#2556](https://github.com/expo/eas-cli/issues/2556)).

**Deliberately NOT changed:** the `expo-location` plugin entry. Do not add
`isAndroidForegroundServiceEnabled` / `isAndroidBackgroundLocationEnabled` unless
device GPS turns out to be needed for its own sake (§1c).

### Runtime shape

```
[user taps Record, app in foreground]
  → request POST_NOTIFICATIONS (Android 13+) — FGS runs without it, but the
    notification is hidden from the drawer (Android docs, notification-permission)
  → check PowerManager.isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations(); warn/route to settings
  → NmeaForegroundService.start({ title, body })   // startForeground(connectedDevice)
       ↳ acquires PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK
       ↳ starts HeadlessJsTask (no timeout, allowedInForeground)
       ↳ starts native 1 Hz tick → emits 'tick' to JS
  → TcpSocket.createConnection({ host, port, interface: 'wifi', localAddress })
  → socket.on('data')  → split on CRLF → parse → in-memory ring buffer
  → on('tick') or every N sentences → batch INSERT into SQLite + append raw log
  → [user taps Stop] → flush → socket.destroy() → service.stop()
```

Note the pipeline is entirely event-driven: `data` events and a *native* tick.
No `setInterval` anywhere.

## Validation plan (do this before building anything)

The gate is "passed with caveats"; the caveats are only retired by a device run.
This does not need the boat — ticket 12's simulator on a laptop over the same
WiFi is a faithful stand-in for the Zeus 3.

1. **Rig the minimum.** Simulator emitting NMEA at the target rate → phone joins
   the laptop's hotspot (mimicking the boat AP: **no internet**, which is the
   condition that makes `interface: 'wifi'` matter).
2. **Spike with `react-native-background-actions@4.1.0`** + the plugin above
   with `foregroundServiceType: ['connectedDevice']`. Fastest path to an answer.
3. **Run 3 hours, screen off, app backgrounded, unplugged.** Log a monotonic
   sentence counter and wall-clock to SQLite so gaps are visible after the fact.
4. **Answer these specific questions:**
   - Does the socket stay open for the full 3 hours? Any silent stalls?
   - **Do JS `setInterval` callbacks fire with the screen off** while the
     Headless JS task is active? (This is the one thing I could not resolve from
     documentation. If yes, the design gets easier; if no, the native tick is
     mandatory — build it either way.)
   - Battery drain over 3 hours, and case temperature.
   - Does it survive with battery optimisation *enabled* on Logan's actual
     phone, or only when excluded?
   - `adb logcat | grep -i "freez\|froze"` — confirm the process is never
     frozen.
5. **Then** port to the local module, and re-run step 3 once as a regression.

If step 4 shows the socket surviving 3 hours, the gate is fully passed and
tickets 04/05/09/11 proceed as mapped. If it dies reliably at some interval,
fallback 1 or 2 from §6 becomes the design.

## Risks

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **OEM battery manager kills the FGS mid-race** | Medium — device-dependent, well documented in the wild | High — lost race data | Battery-optimisation exclusion at onboarding + `isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()` gate; crash-tolerant continuous persistence; keep-awake fallback mode |
| 2 | **JS timers don't fire screen-off even under a Headless JS task** | Unknown (unresolved) | Medium — only if the design used timers | Native tick in the module; strictly event-driven pipeline. Cost of the mitigation is ~10 lines, so just build it. |
| 3 | `react-native-tcp-socket` becomes unmaintained; interop layer eventually removed from RN | Low near-term, Medium at 2-3 year horizon | High | Keep socket usage behind a thin app-side seam so it can be swapped for a native reader inside the service (fallback 3) |
| 4 | `react-native-background-actions` doesn't work on RN 0.83 New Arch | Medium — untested on 0.8x, [#224](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-background-actions/issues/224) history | Low — spike-only dependency | Discover in step 2 of the spike; go straight to the local module |
| 5 | Android routes the socket over cellular / drops the internet-less boat AP | Medium | High — no data at all | `interface: 'wifi'` on connect; test on a hotspot with no upstream internet |
| 6 | `foregroundServiceType` blanked by the manifest merge / EAS | Low | High — instant `InvalidForegroundServiceTypeException` crash on targetSdk 36 | Assert on the generated manifest after prebuild; add a CI/lint check |
| 7 | Wrong FGS type chosen later "for convenience" → Android 15 6h cap | Low | Medium — dies at hour 6, or on a two-race day | Documented here; `connectedDevice` only |
| 8 | Long SQLite write session on the JS thread stutters the capture | Low | Low | Batch inserts in a transaction off the tick, not per sentence |
| 9 | `expo prebuild --clean` wipes manual `android/` edits | High if anyone hand-edits | Medium | Everything native goes through the config plugin or the local module. Never hand-edit `android/`. |

## What I could not establish

Flagged honestly, because the ticket asked for it:

- **Whether `android.view.Choreographer` delivers frame callbacks with the
  display off.** I confirmed from this repo's RN source that JS timers ride on
  Choreographer via `ReactChoreographer` on the UI thread, and that
  `onHostPause` un-arms and `onHeadlessJsTaskStart` re-arms the callback. I
  could **not** find a primary Android source stating whether vsync/frame
  callbacks continue once the display is off. The existence and popularity of
  the `react-native-background-timer` family strongly implies they do not, and
  field reports on RNBA are contradictory (some report hours of screen-off
  timer work, some report minutes). **This is the single most important unknown
  and step 4 of the spike is designed to answer it.** The recommended design
  routes around it entirely.
- **Whether anyone has run `react-native-tcp-socket` for 3 hours screen-off
  under a foreground service on RN 0.8x.** I found strong evidence it *runs* on
  RN 0.83 New Arch ([#232](https://github.com/Rapsssito/react-native-tcp-socket/pull/232))
  and abundant evidence of the general FGS + long-run pattern working, but no
  report of that exact combination. No such report exists to find; the spike is
  the only way.
- **Whether `react-native-background-actions` 4.1.0 builds and functions on RN
  0.83 / AGP 8.12.** Its `android/build.gradle` still uses
  `implementation 'com.facebook.react:react-native:+'` and its library manifest
  still carries a `package=` attribute — both patterns AGP 8 has been tightening
  on. It may build fine (RN's Gradle plugin substitutes the artifact
  coordinate); I have not compiled it. Treat a build failure here as expected
  noise, not a signal about the approach.
- **Actual battery/thermal cost over 3 hours.** Unmeasurable from documentation.
  Already flagged as open in [`map.md`](../map.md).
- **Whether the B&G Zeus 3's WiFi AP does anything unusual** (idle disconnects,
  client limits, DHCP lease behaviour over 3 hours). That's ticket
  [01](../issues/01-zeus-3-nmea-output.md)'s territory, but a 3-hour idle
  timeout on the *plotter's* side would defeat all of the above. Worth adding to
  01's question list if it isn't there.
